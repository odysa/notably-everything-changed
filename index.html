5,885 papers · 10 CS venues · 2016–2026

Notably, everything changed.

We analyzed the full text of 5,885 computer science papers from before LLMs and after GPT-4.

In short
  • Since 2024, CS papers use longer, more formal words and comment on their own results in the same sentence (“…, highlighting its crucial role”).
  • It happened in every field we checked, including papers that have nothing to do with LLMs, and it was not already happening before 2021.
  • Sentence length, paper structure and the strength of claims barely changed. The wording did.

How this was measured, and caveats
Method

How this was measured

Corpus. OSDI, NSDI, MLSys, VLDB, NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR and ACL, each with a 2019–20 baseline and a 2024–26 sample (full proceedings for USENIX venues and MLSys; random samples of 80–200 otherwise). Six venues also have a 2016 control. ACL and ICML have 80 random papers for every year from 2021 to 2024. COLM (founded 2024) and EuroSys (ACM DL blocks scripts) use arXiv versions and are reported separately. NeurIPS uses 2024 because the 2025 main track was not yet posted.

Text. Body prose only: references, captions, headers, appendices and paragraphs under 40 words are dropped. Comparisons stay within a venue, so template differences mostly cancel. Limitations, ethics and impact sections are left out of style counts because venue rules made them mandatory in the later years.

Attribution. A change counts as LLM-era only when it did not happen between 2016 and 2020, breaks after 2022 in the yearly series, and holds in papers that are not about LLMs. Structure changes such as Limitations sections, longer PDFs and more references trace to venue rules and older trends instead.

Caveats. This measures a shift in style, timed with LLMs. It cannot show that any individual paper was written with AI, and nothing here is a claim about a specific paper. Imitation, editing tools and changes in who writes CS papers could all contribute.
