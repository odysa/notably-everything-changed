Notably, everything changed.

Our original 9,472-paper study found a change in academic writing around 2024. A new census of 21,306 NLP conference papers tests the same three wording patterns against each venue’s earlier trend.

Read the new findingsSee the census figuresRead the original paper (PDF)Play the five-round game
In short
  • Later papers more often use phrases such as “…, highlighting …”, stronger words such as “crucial”, and sentences that begin with Additionally or Notably.
  • A new census of ACL, EMNLP and NAACL papers finds all three fixed patterns above their 2016–22 trends in 2024–25. Separate checks in ML, vision, systems, security and arXiv categories also find increases; their comparison periods differ.
  • This is a study of published text, not a test for AI use. The authors, topics, editing tools and timing of revisions may also have changed.

We collected all 21,368 main-conference papers with PDFs from ACL (2016–26), EMNLP (2016–25) and the NAACL years held during that period. After the same minimum-length rule, 21,306 remained. The original 9,472 papers were the discovery sample; the counts here are a separate census and should not be added to that sample.

Above the old trend

In 2024–25, the extra matches per 1,000 words were +1.06 for functional -ing wording, +0.25 for interpretive -ing wording and +0.41 for Additionally/Notably openers.

The same authors changed

Among 762 people who first-authored papers in both 2020–22 and 2024–26, the increases were 58–72% as large as the all-paper changes.

One opener pattern fell in 2026

ACL openers fell from 0.71 to 0.38 per 1,000 words between 2025 and 2026, as registered before the 2026 text was extracted. Functional -ing wording rose further.

The opener decline does not reveal whether authors, models or writing norms changed.
More checks and limits

The excess remains among papers that are not about LLMs, in NAACL on its own, and when Limitations sections are removed. Separate checks found increases in all three patterns in 1,192 CVPR, ICML and NeurIPS papers (2025 against 2020), 3,254 analysed FAST, Security and ATC papers (3,258 in the frame, compared with trend), and 12,215 analysed arXiv first versions across six CS categories (12,600 selected; 376 below the word-count gate and 9 not retrieved).

Across 1,750 same-paper arXiv revision pairs, two patterns increased more in papers revised across late 2022 than in papers revised across late 2020. Shared paragraphs showed no clear difference; the data do not show authors rewriting existing text. A small biology sample did not meet its three-pattern rule. A parser classified 97–98% of the -ing matches as attached clauses in both eras; human annotation is still pending.

These are observational counts in published text. They cannot show whether an individual paper used AI or what caused the community-wide change. The downloadable paper and interactive panels below describe the earlier discovery sample; the current census numeric reproduction download is now available, while its manuscript is being prepared.

What we counted

What do these measures mean?

We count visible wording patterns in the main text of each paper. For phrase counts, we compare how often a pattern appears per 1,000 words so papers of different lengths can be compared.

Comma + -ing phrases

Words after a comma such as “enabling” or “highlighting”. The two word lists count different kinds of these phrases.

Example: “The cache helps, enabling larger batches.” A word match does not verify its grammar or meaning.

Word choice

Formal words such as “crucial” and “pivotal” became more common compared with “important”. We also count a separate list of words such as “seamless” and “robust”.

These are counts of words, not a judgment that one writing style is better.

Confident or cautious language

We compare emphatic words such as “significantly” with cautious words such as “may” and “likely”. The balance shifts toward more emphatic wording.

Signpost openers

We count sentences that start with “Additionally,” or “Notably,”. They tell a reader that another point or a notable result is coming.

Reading ease

A standard formula combines word and sentence length. By this measure, later papers are harder to read: words grew longer even though sentences did not.

Combined style score

We combine nine selected text-pattern counts, including the two comma + -ing lists, the openers, em dashes and “This shows …” phrasing, after comparing papers within the same conference.

It summarizes writing patterns; it cannot identify whether a paper used AI.

Statistics

How sure are we?

The main finding holds across ten conferences and when we account for writing trends already under way. In 1,192 additional papers from three of those conferences, each of three wording patterns we chose in advance also rose. These checks strengthen the observation, but they cannot tell us what caused it.

For researchers: sample sizes, intervals and statistical checks

The corrected analysis uses 9,101 papers from the ten main venues, including 78 VLDB-2016 controls omitted by the original mapping. Earlier interactive feature panels retain the original descriptive calculations. A separately frozen replication selected 1,200 additional CVPR, ICML and NeurIPS papers from 2020 and 2025; 1,192 passed the unchanged length rule. The equal-venue 2025-minus-2020 differences per 1,000 words were +1.480 [1.369, 1.589] for functional -ing matches, +0.178 [0.156, 0.202] for core interpretive -ing matches, and +0.439 [0.397, 0.482] for Additionally/Notably openers. All three fixed interval lower bounds exceeded zero. These new papers are separate from the 9,472-paper corpus; the check does not validate semantic labels, the nine-feature index or AI causation.

Detection

Can a model tell them apart?

Given one earlier paper and one later paper, a model using writing style picks the later one about 92 times in 100 on average across conferences it had not seen. It is sorting publication periods, not detecting AI use. Topics, authors and shared changes over time could also help explain the difference.

For researchers: model evaluation and interactive plots

A classifier using 33 style measures separates 3,949 originally designated earlier and later papers (1,297 pre, 2,652 post): AUC 0.934 with preprocessing fitted inside each fold, and 0.922 on entirely unseen venues. Other main-venue papers primarily inform the yearly trends. On a later-sampled EMNLP check, the fixed model gives era AUC 0.891 [0.836, 0.934] on 158 eligible papers, 79 per era, with no EMNLP tuning or normalization. That interval resamples papers within years and does not capture common-year or author effects. The plots below illustrate the original descriptive model, whose preprocessing used the full earlier sample; its scores are not the corrected evaluation.

These models separate labeled publication cohorts, not authors or writing tools. An earlier-cohort flag or later-cohort miss does not establish whether anyone used an LLM. Even a 1% earlier-cohort flag rate would mark about 20 of 2,000 papers at a large venue; style should never be used to judge an individual paper.

Details

Data, method and terms

The data: 9,472 papers by venue and year
What we analyzed

9,472 papers, analyzed in full

We measured extracted body prose after removing references, captions, tables and appendices. The corpus has 9,472 papers; corrected pooled inference uses 9,101 from the ten main venues. The historical editing check additionally uses a separate public conversation dataset.

How this was measured, and caveats
Method

How this was measured

Corpus. We use ten main venues (OSDI, NSDI, MLSys, VLDB, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, CVPR and ACL). Each has a 2019–20 baseline, a sample for every year from 2021 to 2024, and a 2024–26 comparison sample, and all but MLSys also have 2016–2018 samples to measure the trend before LLMs. For USENIX venues and MLSys we use the full proceedings; for the others we draw random samples of 80–200 papers per year. ICLR papers are arXiv versions whose comment field says they were accepted at ICLR, because OpenReview does not allow bulk downloads; ICLR 2016 has only 29 of them. COLM, founded in 2024, and EuroSys, whose PDFs we could not download in bulk, use arXiv versions and are reported separately. The frozen NeurIPS sample ends in 2024 and does not include 2025.

Text. We keep extracted body prose and drop references, captions, headers, appendices and paragraphs under 40 words. We compare within venue, but templates, source versions and extraction behavior can still change by year. Limitations, ethics and impact sections are excluded from style measures because venue rules made them mandatory during the study period.

Analysis. We examine older trends, the yearly series and papers with few LLM mentions. The original descriptive figures use 9,023 main-venue papers; corrected pooled inference includes the 78 VLDB-2016 controls omitted by that mapping, for 9,101 papers. A whole-venue bootstrap gives a 95% interval of +1.50 to +2.40 pre-period SD around the trend-adjusted +1.97 style-index shift. Feature and lexicon choices were refined while exploring this corpus. Fitting preprocessing inside classifier folds fixes one leakage path, but not optimism from that global feature selection. A later-specified EMNLP sample tests the fixed features and model in one new venue: 160 papers were selected before outcomes, two failed the fixed length gate, and the other 158 yielded era AUC 0.891. Functional and interpretive pattern counts and Additionally/Notably openers all had positive later-minus-earlier gaps. The separate CVPR/ICML/NeurIPS expansion fixed its three count measures and sampling rule before retrieval; 1,200 PDFs were obtained, eight failed the unchanged length gate, and the remaining 1,192 met the frozen three-interval joint criterion. Source IDs did not overlap the original sample, although authors and topics may. The local protocols are prospective for these bounded checks, not external preregistrations; they do not confirm broad cross-field generalization or the nine-feature index effect. The participial-rider measures are word-list and regex proxies: a comma, optional listed adverb and lowercase -ing word after character position 24. They do not verify main-clause attachment or sentence-final position. A separately frozen 80-paper PLOS Genetics check did not pass its joint three-feature criterion: only the functional -ing interval excluded zero positively. A two-agent blind labeling pilot is descriptive, not human gold; the rare core interpretive category was scarcely sampled. These checks narrow explanations; they do not isolate LLM use from shared year effects, changing authors, revisions or other tools.

Editing checks and caveats. Current Claude edits raise boosters and lower hedges but do not reproduce the riders or openers. A separate historical check uses real April 2023–April 2024 ChatGPT editing conversations from the WildChat-1M dataset (Zhao et al., ICLR 2024; pinned dataset revision 7d6490e462285cf85d91eabea0f9a954fbddcd1f). Its 64-pair academic subset is inconclusive; selection was revised three times before feature computation, twice after academic pair counts were seen. The 677 broader English edits reproduce three signature directions under an exploratory analysis. The selection protocol and exclusion counts are included with this site's downloadable research materials. Only aggregate statistics are released, not conversation text. The datasets differ in passages, users and requests. An 11-author, 31-paper ACL panel is too small to control author composition broadly, and publication years cannot establish writing time. Nothing here identifies AI use in a particular paper.

How this project was made. Under the author's direction, AI agents helped write analysis code, draft the paper and website, and review the results. The downloadable code and numeric inputs reproduce the 36 main statistical CSVs and 158 external model scores; upstream PDF extraction and historical WildChat selection need separately obtained public sources.
